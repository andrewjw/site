Recently I made my now annual trek into London for LDX3, the festival for engineering leadership. For the second time
this was held at the [Intercontinental at the O2](https://iclondon-theo2.com/). LDX3 is a huge conference with over
2,600 attendees, three main stages, two smaller "solutions" stages, a wide range of table talks and community meet ups,

<!--more-->

This year some changes had been made which addressed most of the concerns I had - food was plentiful for lunch and all
the breaks, and the stages in general were better. The two smaller tracks - Making Technical Choices and Building For
Production - had moved downstairs where there was more room. These rooms were not perfect, and the Technical Choices
room was very wide and not very deep, which meant you could be a long way off to the side of the stage. The biggest
issues on these stages was that the screens went from floor to ceiling, and given the seating was not raked mean you
couldn't see the bottom half of the screens. LeadDev obviously heard this, and for day two the slides were shrunk and
moved up so it was much easier to see.

![Hard To Read Screen](/assets/ldx3_london_2026_blocked_screen.jpeg)

The Intercontinental is not the easiest to get to (especially for those of us coming from north of London), and is a bit
soulless, but after the teething troubles of last year seems to work well. While I'm happy to see LeadDev be successful,
I kind of hope LDX3 doesn't grow much further as it's already so big, and there is so much going. It's fairly overwhelming
during the breaks.

One change from previous years is that the subtitles were AI generated, instead of being live transcribed by a human. 
I'm lucky enough to not need them, but I do use them occasionally to check back on what was said while writing my
notes. The AI subtitles were mostly fine, but definitely struggled on occasions - particular if the speaker was not
a native English speaker. I also missed the human touch of the stage host interacting with the transcriber at the start
of the day. Maybe I'm just old school though.

In between the talks whoever playlists the music between talks knows exactly what kind of music I like](https://www.last.fm/user/andrew_j_w).
La Roux, Kurt Vile, Field Music and many more. I would absolutely go to a LeadDev DJ gig - it'd be wall to wall bangers!

## The Story Box

_[Michael Lopp](http://www.randsinrepose.com/)_ ([Video](https://leaddev.com/communication/the-story-box))

Michael Lopp is a rock star in the software engineering leadership community. Like a lot of people I have read his
"Rands in Repose" [books](https://randsinrepose.com/books/) and [blog posts](https://randsinrepose.com/), listened
to his [podcast](https://randsinrepose.com/the-important-thing/) and am a (mostly lurking) member of his
[leadership slack](https://randsinrepose.com/welcome-to-rands-leadership-slack/), so it was quite a coup for LDX3
to have him as their opening key note speaker.

Rands/Lopp talked about the art of story telling, and why as a leader it's an important skill to practice. He began
by talking about various components a good story. The cold open, where a lack of context will pull people in the story.
MacGuffins, which are a objects that drive the story forward. Or how to use magic to engage your audience.

The talk then moves on to how to engage people in the story - help them to see themselves in it, become engages and make 
their own story. There are then several interesting lessons about leadership and story telling from various companies
where Michael has worked.

Finally the talk concludes with some points about AI and how it can help and hinder your storytelling.

## The mechanics of scaling: Why delivery slows as you grow, and what to do about it

_[Maryia Tarpachova](http://www.linkedin.com/in/maryiatarpachova)_ ([Video](https://leaddev.com/technical-direction/the-mechanics-of-scaling-why-delivery-slows-as-you-grow-and-what-to-do-about-it))

This talk is about how systems thinking can help with scaling your teams and your software. The talk beings with a story
about how a growing theme park stopped growing. The reason was that the road to the park was clogged. Creating the demand
was not the problem, handling it was. They added a bus service, and the growth resumed.

Growth is a reinforcing loop. Adding features drives growth, which drives investment. But features also drive complexity
and drive cognative load. Growth is not isolated, and you need to balance these two cycles.

Maryia advocates for making deliberate technical tradeoffs, rather than allowing accidental complexity. If you have strong
conventions then entire categories of decisions are removed. If your team is responsible for a domain that is too big then
even the best engineer will struggle.

The best time to resolve and issue is before it becomes a crisis. You should monitor your performance and if it drops without
a clear cause then you've likely hit a constraint.
